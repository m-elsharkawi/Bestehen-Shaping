# Bestehen / App — Stage 08: A1 → B1 Progression Model

> **Status:** Authoritative Stage 08 product-shaping decision
>
> **Product:** Bestehen / App
>
> **Launch target:** TELC B1
>
> **Progression promise:** Start with exam-shaped work an A1 learner can meaningfully attempt, then remove support and add authentic B1 complexity, timing, and constraints only as direct skill-specific evidence justifies it.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document defines how Bestehen / App moves a learner from approximately A1 toward authentic TELC B1 performance without placing a conventional A1 → A2 → B1 course in front of exam preparation.

It defines:

- five evidence-driven progression rungs;
- the unit of progression and the independent difficulty dimensions within it;
- the assisted A1/early-A2 experience;
- all routine assistance types and their evidence consequences;
- how support moves from default, to on request, to unavailable during a measured attempt;
- the transitions into genuine A2, A2/B1 bridge, genuine B1, and target exam conditions;
- when timers, playback restrictions, task sequencing, and other exam constraints appear;
- promotion, provisional promotion, regression, and recovery rules;
- skill-specific examples for Reading, Language Elements, Listening, Writing, and Speaking;
- low-cost operating defaults and later calibration requirements.

This is a product model, not an implementation architecture, screen specification, content-generation system, or final TELC task blueprint. Stages 09–13 own the precise task inventories and skill rubrics; Stages 15–17 own readiness, mocks, and ready-to-book gates. Those stages must preserve the support, evidence, and realism boundaries locked here.

### Core decision

**Progression is a skill-specific realism profile, not a global CEFR level, chapter, unit, or course gate.**

A learner may use target-condition Reading while still receiving guided Listening and scaffolded Speaking. They never need to “finish A2” before seeing B1-shaped tasks. The exam mechanism stays visible from the beginning; language load, task complexity, support, timing, playback, and production independence change separately.

Progress does not mean that assistance becomes permanently forbidden in all learning. It means the learner can produce increasingly strong evidence without that assistance and can eventually perform under the exact conditions claimed.

## 2. Inputs / Existing Decisions

Stage 08 preserves the project overview, identity, and App Stages 01–07:

- Bestehen is a calm, direct, evidence-based personal German exam coach.
- TELC B1 is the only launch target, while cheap seams for future exams may remain.
- A learner may start around A1. The target remains authentic B1 exam performance.
- German is learned through exam-shaped work, not textbook chapter completion.
- The canonical loop is **Observe → Attempt → Diagnose → Repair → Retry → Remember**.
- Readiness is primary, with separate written and oral pass risk.
- The initial diagnostic routes each skill independently and uses Foundation, Bridge to B1, B1 evidence building, and not-enough-evidence language instead of a global CEFR placement.
- The learner model separates capability, confidence, freshness, assistance, timing, direct-evidence lane, and readiness contribution.
- Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking production, and interactive Speaking require direct evidence. One modality cannot initialize another.
- Assistance is recorded by type and use. Help offered but unused does not weaken an attempt; post-response feedback does not retroactively change it.
- Open, practice-timed, and exam-timed evidence remain distinct.
- Mistake Memory requires independent recurrence and delayed fresh transfer. Time and immediate retry do not repair a weakness.
- The Daily Coaching Engine chooses the highest-value reachable work and converts an unreachable high-priority target into a prerequisite, scaffolded attempt, or clarifying task.
- The core loop uses narrow repairs, fresh equivalent retries, and an eight-minute normal repair boundary.
- A measured mock or exam-mode block defers feedback and repair until the block ends.
- Corpus supplies skill/task relationships, difficulty and TELC relevance. The App owns learner progression, assistance choice, and evidence interpretation.
- Routine state changes and coaching explanations should be deterministic and inexpensive.

There are no contradictions with earlier decisions. This stage makes the assistance and realism transitions previously deferred by Stages 02–07 concrete.

## 3. Decisions and Rationale

### 3.1 Progress by task family and capability cluster

**Decision:** The normal progression unit is a **task-pattern × direct-evidence lane profile**, with capability-level exceptions where evidence requires them.

Examples include:

- Reading notices for restrictions and matching;
- Reading longer-text gist and inference;
- Language Elements contextual connector choice;
- Listening announcement details;
- Listening speaker intention;
- Writing functional-message task fulfillment;
- Writing production of a recurring grammar pattern;
- Speaking personal contribution;
- Speaking planning/interaction.

This is finer than one rung per major section and simpler than a rung for every vocabulary word. A major-skill summary may show the typical or highest safely usable profile, but it must not force every task family to the same level.

### 3.2 Difficulty is multidimensional

**Decision:** Each progression profile can change along the following axes:

| Axis | What changes from Foundation to target conditions |
|---|---|
| Linguistic load | Vocabulary frequency, sentence length, grammar density, abstraction, paraphrase, and implicit meaning |
| Task/reasoning load | Number of steps, distractor similarity, inference, matching burden, and required information integration |
| Task length and breadth | Microtask → complete task family → section sequence |
| Assistance | Default visible support → on-request support → no learning help during measured attempt |
| Timing | Open → soft target → generous practice timer → target-time probe → exact target timing |
| Playback/reference | Extra replay, slower audio, transcript/translation, phrase banks, and reference access progressively reduce |
| Production independence | Selection/completion/frames → guided production → open original production |
| Realism and sequencing | Isolated task → mixed task forms → target order, one-chance decisions, endurance, and deferred feedback |
| Evaluation standard | Success at working difficulty → B1-sufficient rubric behavior → target-condition evidence |

Promotion can change one axis without changing the others. For example, Listening may move from slowed to normal audio while keeping one extra replay, or Writing may remove sentence starters while remaining untimed. This makes the transition diagnosable and reversible.

### 3.3 Five progression rungs

| Rung | Learner experience | Language/task profile | Assistance/constraints | Evidence claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P0 — Supported Foundation** | Exam-shaped microtasks are understandable and achievable for A1/early-A2 ability | Short, concrete, high-frequency language; one main reasoning move; reduced output | Relevant support visible by default; open time; slower/careful audio; generous replay; guided production | What can be done with substantial support at accessible difficulty |
| **P1 — Guided A2** | Complete small A2 tasks with familiar exam mechanisms | Genuine A2 sentences/texts/audio; more paraphrase and distractors; short functional output | Support becomes selective or on request; soft timing appears; replay is bounded; fewer production frames | Functional performance at A2 working difficulty and emerging independence |
| **P2 — A2/B1 Bridge** | B1 task patterns are used with controlled language and limited support | Mixed A2/B1 language; authentic reasoning mechanism; longer texts/audio/output; closer distractors | Most support is on request; visible generous timers; playback approaches target rule; open production grows | Transfer from A2 language capability into B1-shaped work |
| **P3 — Independent B1 Training** | Genuine B1 tasks are solved independently in normal practice, with target-condition probes | Authentic B1 language density, task length, distractors, inference, open Writing/Speaking | No default answer-bearing help; target timing/playback introduced in explicit probes; optional practice help marks evidence assisted | Current B1 capability plus accumulating unassisted/timed evidence |
| **P4 — Target Conditions** | The measured attempt behaves like the relevant TELC B1 task or section | Target difficulty, format, length, order, and evaluation standard | Exact target timing/playback/reference rules; no coaching during attempt; diagnosis/repair after the block | Strong target-condition evidence for readiness, mocks, and consistency |

These are not course modules. A strong learner may begin at P2 or P3 after the diagnostic. A learner can choose P4 early for a baseline. A learner who needs P0 in one task family is not globally labeled “P0.”

### 3.4 Adapt assistance, not the goal

The target task pattern should remain recognizable at every rung. Simplification may change surface language, length, number of distractors, and required output, but it must preserve a defensible relationship to the target skill.

Examples:

- an A1 learner can match a simple appointment need to a short notice before handling a full B1 matching set;
- a learner can hear one short appointment announcement with vocabulary preview before handling normal-speed mixed-speaker audio;
- a learner can produce two guided content points in a short message before writing a complete B1 functional response;
- a learner can respond to one fixed partner proposal with a phrase bank before completing a longer planning exchange.

A task that becomes ordinary unrelated language instruction is outside this progression model. A prerequisite repair is allowed, but it must return to an exam-relevant use.

## 4. The Assisted A1 / Early-A2 Experience

### 4.1 Product behavior

At P0, Bestehen should feel accessible without hiding the exam destination.

The learner receives:

- short exam-shaped tasks with one clear objective;
- simple German target content in familiar functional domains such as appointments, transport, housing, work, shopping, services, family, and schedules;
- instructions clarified in the learner’s chosen explanation language when needed;
- a format demonstration before a new task family;
- targeted vocabulary preview rather than translation of everything;
- on-demand word translation and sentence explanation;
- slow but natural audio and multiple recorded playback opportunities;
- constrained Writing/Speaking frames that still require a meaningful learner choice or response;
- neutral language when a target is too hard: “We’ll build this skill with more support,” not repeated failure messaging;
- a fresh check after repair and a clear explanation of which support was used.

P0 does not:

- present a full B1 task merely to establish that it is too hard;
- translate all German by default when a smaller preview makes the attempt possible;
- let model answers masquerade as learner performance;
- force timers or first-play Listening evidence before the format and language are accessible;
- make the learner complete an A1 syllabus before moving an individual skill forward.

### 4.2 What remains authentically exam-shaped

Even with simple language, the learner practices behaviors that later earn points:

- identify a need and match it to a notice;
- find a date, restriction, reason, or change;
- choose a connector or word that completes meaning in context;
- understand gist or one decisive audio detail;
- cover required content points in a functional message;
- respond, give a reason, propose, agree/disagree, or offer an alternative;
- check an instruction, content point, negation, or time constraint.

The product teaches vocabulary and grammar because they unlock these behaviors.

## 5. Assistance Taxonomy and Evidence Treatment

### 5.1 Availability states

Assistance has four product states:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Default** | Visible or applied automatically because the current profile predicts it is needed |
| **On request** | Available before submission if the learner chooses it; use changes the evidence condition when answer-bearing |
| **Repair only** | Not available during the clean attempt; available after submission to diagnose or teach |
| **Unavailable in measured attempt** | Excluded by P4/target rules; may still be used after the measured block |

Fading normally follows **Default → On request → Repair only → unavailable during P4 attempt**. Removing a support from default view is not the same as deleting access. This avoids abrupt jumps and lets the learner demonstrate independence voluntarily.

### 5.2 Assistance classes

| Assistance type | Product behavior | Evidence consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Format demo / instruction clarification | Shows mechanics using unrelated content or explains what the task asks | Non-answer-bearing access support; no penalty unless it reveals live-item reasoning |
| Word translation | Translates a selected word/short phrase | Assisted evidence for vocabulary/comprehension nodes affected by the translation |
| Sentence explanation | Explains the meaning/structure of a live sentence without directly selecting the answer where possible | Assisted comprehension; if it reveals the decisive relation, answer-narrowing evidence |
| Grammar hint | Gives a practical cue or rule relevant to the live item | Assisted grammar/task evidence; exact answer-form hints are weaker than general cues |
| Vocabulary preview | Provides a small set of high-value words before the task | Valid assisted performance; previewed items do not prove independent recognition/recall |
| Slowed/careful audio | Reduces speed or uses a carefully articulated version | Valid Listening evidence at that audio condition, not authentic-speed evidence |
| Replay | Adds playback beyond the target task’s rule | Valid assisted Listening evidence; every play is recorded |
| Transcript/excerpt | Shows written audio content after or, rarely, during a learning attempt | Repair/diagnostic support; performance after exposure does not prove direct Listening independence |
| Strategy cue | Reminds the learner to scan restrictions, check content points, or plan time | Assisted strategy/task evidence |
| Answer-narrowing hint | Highlights evidence location, removes a distractor, supplies partial structure, or gives a decisive cue | Weak learning evidence for independence; useful to make a repair attempt possible |
| Phrase bank / sentence starters | Provides selectable language for Writing or Speaking | Assisted production; spontaneous productive availability remains unproven |
| Planning frame / checklist | Structures content points, Speaking turns, or checking behavior | Assisted strategy evidence when supplied by the App; self-created use may be valid strategy |
| Model response / direct solution | Shows a full answer, transcript, or ideal response | Repair exposure only; revision or immediate repetition is not independent mastery |
| Accessibility support | Text scaling, screen reader, non-answer-bearing extra interaction time, captions where not testing Listening, or input accommodation | Does not weaken ability evidence unless it changes the capability being tested; condition is preserved |

Only support used before the submitted response changes that attempt. Support offered but unused does not. Post-response repair never changes the original attempt retroactively.

### 5.3 Assistance matrix by rung

Legend: **D** = default/visible; **R** = on request; **Post** = repair/diagnosis after the clean attempt; **—** = unavailable during the measured attempt. Exact target-permitted resources are controlled by the task blueprint.

| Assistance | P0 Supported Foundation | P1 Guided A2 | P2 A2/B1 Bridge | P3 Independent B1 Training | P4 Target Conditions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Format demo / instruction clarification | D for new formats | D once, then R | R | R for format only | Target instructions only; access support preserved |
| Word translation | D for selected key words; R elsewhere | R | R, no automatic display | R in practice; Post in target probes | — during attempt; Post |
| Sentence explanation | D/R when task would otherwise be inaccessible | R | R | Post by default; R makes practice assisted | — during attempt; Post |
| Grammar hint | D/R for diagnosed target | R | R | R in practice; Post in probes | — during attempt; Post |
| Vocabulary preview | D, small and targeted | D only for new/high-load topic; otherwise R | Rare default; R | No default; R makes practice assisted | — during attempt |
| Slowed/careful audio | D when needed | R or coach-selected | R; normal speed increasingly default | Rare R in practice; none in target probe | —; target audio only |
| Replay beyond target rule | Generous bounded replay | Up to two extra plays | Target-rule count plus one extra | Target rule by default; one explicit extra in practice marks assisted | —; exact target rule |
| Transcript/excerpt | Post; during attempt only for an explicit non-Listening learning goal | Post | Post | Post | Post measured block only |
| Strategy cue | D for new behavior | D/R | R | R in practice; Post in probes | — during attempt |
| Answer-narrowing hint | After a meaningful attempt or clear wall | R after attempt | R after attempt | Repair only by default | Post only |
| Phrase bank / sentence starters | D for productive tasks | D/R | R, progressively reduced | R in practice; Post in probes | — during attempt |
| Planning frame / checklist | D | D/R | R | Learner-generated strategy preferred; App frame makes practice assisted | Only target-permitted learner-created notes |
| Model response / direct solution | Post | Post | Post | Post | Post measured block |

### 5.4 Fading order

Support fades by evidence and causal relevance, not by a universal calendar. The normal sequence is:

1. remove direct solution/model exposure from any pre-response path;
2. remove answer-narrowing hints and partial answers;
3. change sentence explanations, grammar cues, phrase banks, and strategy prompts from default to on request;
4. reduce automatic vocabulary preview and word translation;
5. restore normal audio speed and reduce extra playback;
6. remove on-request answer-bearing help from explicit target probes;
7. add target timing, task sequencing, and full P4 constraints.

This order is a default. The diagnosed blocker controls the actual move. If audio speed is the only problem, vocabulary preview can already be absent while slower audio remains. If Writing language is functional but planning is weak, the timer may advance while a non-answer-bearing planning frame remains in ordinary practice.

Accessibility support does not fade merely to make a task look realistic. Stage 25 will decide which accommodations preserve a target-condition claim.

## 6. Promotion and Assistance-Fading Rules

### 6.1 The evidence cycle

Progression uses a four-step cycle:

1. **Stabilize:** collect fresh evidence at the current profile.
2. **Probe:** change one meaningful axis—remove one support, increase one difficulty dimension, reduce playback, or add a timer.
3. **Adopt provisionally:** use the harder profile for the next suitable activity if the probe succeeds.
4. **Confirm or recover:** confirm with a later fresh success, or restore the smallest needed support and diagnose the wall.

One result can open a provisional profile; it cannot establish stable progression by itself.

### 6.2 Eligibility for a harder probe

A task family becomes eligible for a one-axis progression probe when:

- the learner understands the task mechanism;
- there is no unresolved technical/content problem;
- current-profile evidence is at least Functional with medium confidence for the claim being advanced;
- objective work normally includes at least **three fresh relevant opportunities across at least two sessions**, with about **70% or better** aggregate success or at least two clearly successful clusters and no repeated unaddressed blocker;
- a productive/interactive target normally includes at least **two fresh direct samples across two occasions** that meet the current working standard for the dimension being advanced;
- at least one recent success used no answer-narrowing help;
- content at the proposed next profile is available and reachable;
- a higher-priority repair obligation does not make the probe wasteful today.

The percentages and counts are initial routing defaults, not readiness thresholds. Rich productive tasks use fewer samples because each response contains multiple directly evaluated behaviors and carries greater time/cost.

### 6.3 What a probe may change

A normal probe changes **one primary axis**:

- remove vocabulary preview but keep open time;
- remove sentence starters but keep a phrase bank on request;
- use normal-speed audio but keep one extra replay;
- keep language/task complexity stable and add a generous timer;
- keep time open and increase distractor/task complexity;
- keep a B1 task untimed but remove all pre-response help;
- run the same task family under target playback/timing as an explicit evidence probe.

A probe may include inseparable linked changes only when the target task would otherwise be invalid—for example, a measured Listening probe may need the target playback rule and continuous audio together. The product explains what changed.

### 6.4 Provisional adoption and confirmation

- A successful fresh probe moves the relevant profile to **provisionally adopted**.
- A second fresh success on another occasion at the new condition confirms the profile for routine coaching.
- If the learner uses the faded support on the provisional attempt, the result remains valid assisted evidence but does not confirm independence.
- A correct result caused by model exposure, repeated content, or a near-duplicate does not promote.
- A promotion in one task family does not automatically promote the whole section.
- Strong diagnostic evidence may start a learner directly at P2/P3 without replaying lower rungs.
- P4 is not “unlocked” permanently. It is an evidence condition that may be selected whenever a valid measured task is useful.

### 6.5 Learner choice

The coach recommends the next profile, but the learner may:

- request support during ordinary practice;
- choose a harder profile or P4 baseline early;
- choose a supported practice after a difficult day;
- decline a timer, audio, or microphone context;
- return to a familiar profile for repair.

Using support is not failure. It changes the evidence claim and may keep the profile provisional. Repeated learner-selected support becomes evidence of support dependence only when comparable performance supports that conclusion.

## 7. Transition Rules Between Complexity Bands

### 7.1 Into genuine A2 complexity

P0 → P1 occurs within a task family when the learner can make meaningful attempts with less default support.

Genuine A2 means:

- complete simple functional sentences/texts/audio rather than isolated words;
- more varied high-frequency vocabulary and basic connectors;
- at least one plausible distractor or competing detail;
- short complete task forms rather than only demonstrations;
- short independent output, even if limited;
- bounded rather than open-ended replay;
- first soft timing goals after the task format is understood.

The learner does not need comprehensive A1 grammar or vocabulary coverage. The transition is earned by functional use in the exam-shaped task.

### 7.2 Into A2/B1 bridge material

P1 → P2 occurs when genuine A2 work is functional and the learner benefits more from the B1 task mechanism than from additional same-level repetition.

Bridge material uses:

- authentic or near-authentic B1 task patterns;
- A2-to-early-B1 language with selected B1 vocabulary/grammar;
- longer information spans and closer paraphrases;
- distractors based on restrictions, speaker changes, required content, or intention;
- open but bounded Writing/Speaking;
- most assistance on request rather than always visible;
- a generous visible practice timer;
- playback closer to the target rule.

Bridge success is not B1 readiness. Its purpose is to transfer accessible language into the reasoning and production patterns the exam rewards.

### 7.3 Into genuine B1 complexity

P2 → P3 occurs when the learner can handle the task mechanism with limited support and direct evidence shows that controlled language, not mere format confusion, is no longer the dominant constraint.

Genuine B1 means:

- natural B1 sentence and discourse density;
- normal paraphrase, synonym, distractor, and inference demands;
- target-like task length and required content breadth;
- normal-speed, varied-speaker audio appropriate to the task;
- open original Writing/Speaking rather than selection or completion;
- B1-sufficient communicative evaluation, not perfectionist native-level correction;
- no default answer-bearing assistance;
- explicit unassisted and target-condition probes.

A learner may reach P3 for gist Listening while remaining P1 for numbers/dates recognition. The coach continues to select the precise task family.

### 7.4 Into target conditions

P3 → P4 is a change in **measurement conditions**, not a graduation ceremony.

P4 requires:

- the target task blueprint and format;
- genuine B1 difficulty;
- exact or explicitly defined target timing;
- exact target playback, reference, pause, and preparation rules;
- no translation, hints, transcript, model, or coach intervention during the attempt;
- target-length output/sequence as applicable;
- feedback, diagnosis, repair, and fresh retry only after the measured task or block ends;
- clear recording that the task was complete, continuous, valid, and unassisted.

The coach recommends P4 when P3 evidence is stable enough that a realistic attempt is more informative than more supported practice, when a mock/readiness decision needs stronger evidence, or when a booked date makes a baseline urgent. The learner may choose it earlier.

## 8. Timing, Playback, Retry, and Exam Constraints

### 8.1 Timer progression

| Rung | Timer behavior |
|---|---|
| P0 | No performance countdown. Time may be observed privately for planning; accessibility and comprehension take priority |
| P1 | Soft, non-blocking target appears after the learner understands the task; normally around 150% of target time where a target exists |
| P2 | Visible generous practice timer, normally around 125–150% of target; running out of time diagnoses pace but does not erase language evidence |
| P3 | Target-time probes begin. Routine practice may use about 110–125% until stable, then target time becomes the normal unassisted condition |
| P4 | Exact target timing and continuous-task rules; pauses or extensions are recorded and cannot be called unchanged target-condition evidence |

The percentages are initial product defaults and may be tuned per task by Stages 09–13. A timer is introduced only after the learner understands the format well enough that it measures execution rather than onboarding confusion.

For Writing and Speaking, preparation time and response time are distinct. For section sequences, endurance and allocation appear after isolated task timing is usable.

### 8.2 Audio speed and replay progression

| Rung | Audio speed | Playback |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Carefully articulated natural speech or approximately 0.8–0.9× when technically acceptable | Initial play plus up to three extra learner-controlled plays; beyond that, move to diagnosis/transcript repair rather than loop indefinitely |
| P1 | Approximately 0.9–1.0×, with normal speed used when reachable | Initial play plus up to two extra plays |
| P2 | Normal/near-normal speech by default | Target-rule play count plus one explicit extra play |
| P3 | Authentic normal speed | Target-rule count by default; one extra may be requested in ordinary practice and marks the result assisted; target probes allow no extra |
| P4 | Target audio | Exact target-rule count; no extra replay, rewind, transcript, or unpermitted pause |

Prefer natural careful recordings or reviewed audio variants to badly distorted slow playback. The exact TELC playback count is task-specific and must come from the target blueprint defined by the Listening and Mock stages; Stage 08 locks when that rule becomes authoritative.

### 8.3 Learning retries versus exam attempts

Two concepts must not be confused:

- **Audio replay** is another exposure to the same Listening content.
- **Learning retry** is fresh equivalent content after diagnosis/repair.

At P0–P3 ordinary coaching, the Stage 07 fresh retry remains available after a shortfall. Support follows the current profile and is recorded.

At P3 target probes and all P4 measured attempts:

- no between-item diagnosis, repair, or fresh retry occurs inside the measured block;
- submitted answers remain final under the applicable rules;
- feedback is deferred;
- later repair uses separate content;
- a subsequent fresh task may test transfer, but it is not retroactively part of the measured attempt.

Thus retries become exam-realistic when measured P3 probes begin and are fully target-realistic at P4.

### 8.4 Constraint sequence

Exam constraints normally appear in this order:

1. task mechanism and response format from P0;
2. complete small tasks and bounded replay at P1;
3. visible generous timing, longer tasks, and closer playback rules at P2;
4. individual target-timed/playback probes and authentic B1 task length at P3;
5. consecutive target tasks, section order, deferred feedback, endurance, and exact rules at P4/mocks.

A learner with a booked near-term exam may compress the sequence, but the evidence must state exactly which conditions were met.

## 9. Regression, Difficulty Walls, and Recovery

### 9.1 What counts as a difficulty wall

A wall is considered when comparable valid evidence shows one or more of:

- about one-third or fewer correct across a small objective cluster;
- two consecutive no-meaningful-response events after the task is understood;
- two failed fresh checks in one loop, as already limited by Stage 07;
- repeated failure across two occasions after a provisional promotion;
- productive work cannot meet the minimum task behavior even with light support;
- the learner repeatedly needs answer-narrowing/model support to produce a meaningful response;
- performance collapses only under time, playback, task length, or fatigue conditions.

One ordinary mistake is not a wall. Technical failure, ambiguous content, inaccessible interaction, or unreliable evaluation must be excluded first.

### 9.2 Recovery sequence

When a wall appears:

1. **Validate:** exclude technical, content, instruction, and evaluator problems.
2. **Locate the axis:** language, task reasoning, output breadth, assistance, speed, playback, timing, fatigue, or strategy.
3. **Restore one support or reduce one axis:** do not globally demote the skill.
4. **Repair the smallest prerequisite:** keep it exam-relevant.
5. **Use a fresh check at the reachable profile.**
6. **Record the difference:** which support restored meaningful performance and which target remains open.
7. **Re-plan:** keep the learner at the restored working profile and schedule a later one-axis probe.

Examples:

- normal-speed audio fails but 0.9× succeeds: keep normal speed as the target, practise at 0.9×, and probe normal speed later; do not lower general vocabulary automatically;
- full Writing task fails because content points are omitted: shorten the response and restore a planning frame; do not restart grammar;
- target timer causes incompletion while untimed language is B1-sufficient: keep B1 language difficulty and step timing back to a generous practice target;
- B1 inference is unreachable but detail scanning works: keep Reading task-family profiles separate.

### 9.3 Temporary fallback versus profile regression

| Situation | Product response |
|---|---|
| One weak/contradictory result | Keep the current profile, lower confidence if material, and use a clarifying fresh task |
| Same-session wall | Temporary one-axis fallback for repair; no permanent regression |
| Failed provisional promotion on another fresh task | Return the changed axis to the prior confirmed profile and schedule a later probe |
| Repeated comparable failure across at least two occasions | Regress the relevant axis/task family one step until repair/transfer succeeds |
| One strong P4 failure against stable P3 evidence | Treat as a target-condition blocker/contradiction; diagnose timing, strategy, fatigue, or language before broad regression |
| Repeated valid P4 failure | Let realistic evidence dominate older supported success and lower the relevant target-condition profile/readiness contribution |
| Long absence | Mark evidence stale and recheck; do not demote from silence alone |
| Learner asks for support on a low-energy day | Use supported practice and record context; do not infer regression |

Regression preserves history. The coach says which condition is being restored and why. It never says the learner has returned to A1 as a person.

### 9.4 Recovery language

Use:

> Normal-speed audio is still the target. Today it blocked a meaningful attempt, so we’ll use one slower version, repair the number contrast, and check normal speed again later.

Avoid:

> You failed B1 Listening and have been moved back to A2.

## 10. Skill-Specific Progression Examples

### 10.1 Progression matrix

| Area | P0 Supported Foundation | P1 Guided A2 | P2 A2/B1 Bridge | P3 Independent B1 Training | P4 Target Conditions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reading | One short notice/message; key words previewed; simple matching/detail | Complete short A2 texts; one or two distractors; translations on request | B1 task mechanism with controlled language, paraphrases, restrictions, and longer sets | Genuine B1 texts/task lengths, normal distractors/inference, no default help | Target format, section timing, sequencing, and no coaching |
| Language Elements | Short contextual choices with practical grammar cues | A2 cloze/context clusters; hints on request | B1-style cloze mechanism with A2/B1 grammar and vocabulary | Genuine B1 density and distractor quality; unassisted/timed probes | Target task, timing, response rules, and deferred feedback |
| Listening | One short clear speaker; vocabulary preview; slower audio; generous replay | Genuine A2 clips, bounded replay, simple gist/detail | Normal-speed transition, multiple details/speakers, target count plus one replay | Authentic B1 audio, target playback probes, paraphrase/intention | Exact target audio, playback, sequencing, and no transcript/help |
| Writing | Choose/complete phrases, sentence starters, two content points, open time | Short functional message with phrase bank/checklist | Full task mechanism, fewer frames, more content points, generous timer | Original B1-sufficient response, no default language support, target-time probes | Exact prompt, preparation/time/length conditions, no model/help |
| Speaking | Fixed personal turn or proposal response; phrase bank; generous preparation | Short exchanges and reasons with limited prompts | Planning/alternative turns with reduced scaffolding and longer response | Genuine B1 monologue/interaction tasks, normal-speed partner behavior, target prep/time probes | Target part sequence, preparation/response rules, continuous measured interaction |

### 10.2 Worked example — Reading restrictions

1. **P0:** Match a simple opening-hours need to three short notices. “werktags” is previewed; word translation is available.
2. **P1:** Match an appointment need to four A2 notices. Translation moves to on request; a soft timer is shown.
3. **P2:** Use a B1-style matching mechanism with controlled-length notices and restrictions such as “nur” and “außer.” No preview; on-request translation marks evidence assisted.
4. **P3:** Complete a genuine B1 text set with close paraphrases, exceptions, and a target-time probe.
5. **P4:** Complete the relevant target task/section under exact timing with no hints. Diagnosis occurs afterward.

If P3 fails because the timer causes rushed checking but untimed accuracy remains strong, timing steps back while B1 language stays.

### 10.3 Worked example — Listening numbers and changes

1. **P0:** Hear one carefully spoken appointment time with key context previewed and up to four total plays.
2. **P1:** Hear a short A2 announcement at near-normal speed with up to three total plays.
3. **P2:** Hear normal-speed announcements with a correction or distractor; use the target play count plus one.
4. **P3:** Use authentic B1 audio under the target playback rule in a probe. An optional extra play is available only outside the probe and produces assisted evidence.
5. **P4:** Use exact task playback and continuous rules. No transcript or intervention until the measured block ends.

If “fünfzehn/fünfzig” fails, the coach uses a short contrastive repair. Written recognition does not promote Listening; a new audio check is required.

### 10.4 Worked example — Writing task fulfillment

1. **P0:** Complete a short functional message from sentence starters covering two explicit points.
2. **P1:** Write a short A2 message with a phrase bank and checklist; no hard timer.
3. **P2:** Write against a B1-shaped prompt with all content points, only optional phrases, and a generous timer.
4. **P3:** Write an original B1-sufficient response without default help; introduce target-time probes.
5. **P4:** Write under exact prompt/time/reference rules. Feedback follows submission.

If the learner’s language is adequate but time causes an omitted point, retain P3 language complexity and restore a planning/checking scaffold or generous timer. Do not demote grammar broadly.

### 10.5 Worked example — Speaking interaction

1. **P0:** Respond to one fixed proposal using a visible phrase bank and generous preparation.
2. **P1:** Give a reason and answer a fixed follow-up with limited starters.
3. **P2:** Complete a short planning exchange with a new constraint; phrases are on request.
4. **P3:** Complete genuine B1 interaction tasks with no default support and target preparation/turn-time probes.
5. **P4:** Complete the target part sequence with target preparation, partner behavior, timing, and no coaching during the measured exchange.

A successful monologue cannot promote interaction. The new profile requires direct response to a partner turn.

## 11. Edge Cases / Failure or Uncertainty Handling

| Situation | Progression behavior |
|---|---|
| Learner is far below the first task | Stop hard probes, use one simpler scaffolded confirmation, and retain the target relationship |
| Learner is already near B1 | Start at P2/P3 and collect target-condition evidence early; do not force lower rungs |
| Skills are uneven | Maintain independent profiles and explain the strongest and weakest conditions |
| One profile contains mixed subskills | Split the relevant task family/capability only when it changes coaching; avoid unnecessary fragmentation |
| Evidence is sparse | Keep the profile provisional and choose the smallest useful evidence task |
| Correct result relied on help | Record assisted success; do not promote the faded support |
| Learner requests a hint after struggling | Provide it in ordinary practice, record the support, and use a later fresh unassisted check |
| Promotion causes frustration or blank responses | Restore one support immediately and diagnose the changed axis |
| Same item is repeated | Reduce/exclude independence; do not use it for promotion |
| Content pool lacks a valid next rung | Use a broader reviewed equivalent or keep the profile stable; do not serve low-quality generated content |
| Audio cannot be slowed naturally | Use a reviewed careful recording or smaller excerpt during repair; avoid distorted evidence |
| Timer is inaccessible or accommodation is needed | Preserve the accommodation and separate it from ordinary slowness; Stage 25 defines target-condition treatment |
| Microphone is unavailable | Keep oral profile/evidence incomplete; typed rehearsal does not promote Speaking |
| AI evaluation is pending/uncertain | Do not promote unsupported productive dimensions; use a new sample or lower confidence |
| Learner returns after months | Preserve historical profiles, mark evidence stale, and run a few high-value rechecks |
| Learner chooses P4 early and performs poorly | Treat it as diagnostic target-condition evidence and build a reachable plan; no punishment |
| Booked date is too close for gradual progression | Compress probes, prioritize recoverable points and exam execution, and state risk honestly |
| Strong supported practice conflicts with repeated P4 failure | Keep working capability visible but let recent valid target evidence control readiness/target profile |
| Assistance is used for confidence rather than necessity | Compare fresh assisted/unassisted evidence before declaring dependence |
| A task uses protected reference material | Rights rules still apply; authentic difficulty does not require verbatim official content |

### Support cannot become a permanent comfort loop

If the learner repeatedly succeeds only at a supported profile, the coach should not keep serving identical comfortable work. It alternates:

- reachable supported work;
- one-axis independence probes;
- narrow repairs for the blocker;
- honest explanation that training is improving but target-condition evidence is still missing.

The learner retains the right to choose supported practice, but the readiness model must not interpret it as P4 capability.

## 12. Validation and Recalibration

The counts, success heuristics, timing ratios, audio-speed bands, and playback steps in this document are initial product defaults. They preserve uncertainty rather than claim scientific calibration.

Stage 26 should evaluate:

- success rate on the first attempt after promotion;
- fallback/regression rate within the next three comparable attempts;
- learner abandonment or repeated no-meaningful-response after a probe;
- assistance-request rate before and after fading;
- delayed transfer after support removal;
- P2/P3 performance as a predictor of P4 target-condition performance;
- time from supported to independent performance by task family;
- false promotion, unnecessary delay, and oscillation between profiles;
- differences for productive versus objective tasks;
- whether timers measure skill rather than unfamiliarity;
- readiness calibration by the realism of evidence.

A progression default should change only when transfer and target-condition outcomes improve, not merely because harder tasks increase completion speed or app engagement.

## 13. Relationship to Other Bestehen Stages

- **Stage 03 — Initial Diagnostic:** chooses the first provisional profile per skill and must keep its confidence caps.
- **Stage 04 — Learner Model:** stores difficulty, assistance, timing, playback, evidence lane, provisional/confirmed condition, and target-condition summaries.
- **Stage 05 — Mistake Memory:** supplies confirmed blockers, repair status, and delayed transfer; support fading does not override weakness lifecycle gates.
- **Stage 06 — Daily Coaching Engine:** decides when a progression probe is the highest-value reachable activity and converts walls into prerequisites or supported work.
- **Stage 07 — Core Learning Loop:** owns attempt/diagnosis/repair/fresh retry semantics. Stage 08 supplies the difficulty and support profile used in each state.
- **Stages 09–13 — Skill Coaching:** define exact TELC task maps, skill-specific success rubrics, content requirements, target times, playback counts, and which assistance changes each evidence claim.
- **Stage 14 — Review:** schedules delayed independence and target-condition checks without creating a separate flashcard course.
- **Stage 15 — TELC Readiness:** must weight P4 and recent P3 target probes much more strongly than supported P0–P2 work while still recognizing learning progress.
- **Stage 16 — Mock Exams:** provides multi-task P4 measurement, sequencing, stamina, and exact constraints; mocks are not progression unlocks.
- **Stage 17 — Ready to Book:** requires sufficient recent target-condition written and oral evidence; no collection of assisted successes can substitute.
- **Stage 18 — Coach Personality:** defines the exact bilingual explanation policy and wording as German gradually increases; this stage requires support-state clarity without shame.
- **Stage 19 — Information Architecture:** may display a simple journey summary, but must preserve skill-specific support/realism detail and make available help clear before an attempt.
- **Stage 20 — Progress and Motivation:** may recognize “less help needed” and “first target-condition success” as meaningful milestones, not arbitrary levels.
- **Stage 21 — Content Strategy:** supplies reviewed variants across rungs, fresh equivalence, difficulty metadata, audio variants, and anti-repetition controls.
- **Stage 22 — Corpus → App Contract:** supplies versioned difficulty/relevance/task-pattern intelligence, including A1-assisted, A2, A2/B1, and B1 use cases.
- **Stage 24 — Account/Data:** preserves profile/evidence history and support use according to the data contract.
- **Stage 25 — Accessibility/Device:** decides which accommodations preserve target-condition claims and ensures timers/audio/input do not create false regression.
- **Stage 26 — Analytics:** recalibrates progression thresholds from transfer, target performance, learner friction, and outcome data.
- **Stage 27 — Cost Boundaries:** sets portfolio limits; routine progression selection remains deterministic.

### Cross-stage guardrails

Later stages may refine task-specific thresholds, but they must preserve:

- no global course level as the primary progression mechanism;
- task-family/direct-lane progression and uneven skill profiles;
- separate axes for language, task, assistance, timing, playback, production, and sequencing;
- Default → On request → Repair only → unavailable-in-measured-attempt fading;
- one-axis probes and provisional promotion;
- no promotion from repeated/exposed or unsupported evidence;
- temporary fallback before persistent regression;
- time and target constraints introduced only after the task is understood;
- direct modality evidence, especially Listening and Speaking;
- exact target conditions as an evidence mode, not a permanent reward;
- honest distinction between supported learning progress and readiness evidence.

## 14. Cost Notes

Stage 08 has **no meaningful recurring premium-AI requirement**.

### Preferred low-cost default

- choose progression profiles, probe eligibility, support fading, timers, replay limits, provisional promotion, and fallback deterministically from stored evidence;
- assemble learner-facing explanations from reviewed templates and structured reasons;
- pre-author or batch-generate reviewed activity families across P0–P4 rather than generate every progression task live;
- reuse target-pattern metadata and difficulty relationships from Corpus;
- use cached audio plus reviewed careful/normal variants or safe playback controls;
- return all Writing/Speaking rubric and assistance information in the single evaluation already needed for the response;
- keep uncertain progression broad/provisional rather than purchasing false precision;
- use a broader reviewed next-rung task when an exact fine-grained variant is unavailable.

### When higher cost could be justified

A higher-quality evaluation or content-generation path is justified only when:

1. a new valid productive sample materially controls movement into P3/P4, a mock, readiness, or ready-to-book decision;
2. the cheaper evaluation cannot support the required rubric distinction;
3. reviewed fallback content cannot test the capability responsibly;
4. the result is stored and reused.

Premium AI is never required to decide whether a word translation was used, a timer was met, an audio replay occurred, or a deterministic answer was correct.

A later upgrade should be driven by measured false promotion, poor transfer, unsafe productive evaluation, or inadequate content coverage—not by a desire to make the coach appear generative.
